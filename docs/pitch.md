# Brainy — US-first hackathon pitch

A ten-slide outline and a three-minute script. The owner builds the deck. Product evidence: [demo walkthrough](demo.md), [mechanics](product/mechanics.md), [current status](progress/status.md). Demonstrate on a fresh jury family. Use real screenshots; label illustrative charts and accelerated placeholder media.

## Three-minute speaker script

Aim for about 18 seconds per slide. Rehearse at roughly 125 words per minute; pause briefly on the product demonstration.

**1 · 0:00–0:18**
“Brainy is a learning companion for children ages four to ten and their parents. We are starting with a US-first, English experience. The child sees a growing character; the parent sees activities they can understand and guide.”

**2 · 0:18–0:36**
“Our starting question is simple: what should the next tap lead to? We offer a bounded learning activity, a visible result and a stopping point. There is no autoplay, no watch-next prompt, and no ranking shown to children.”

**3 · 0:36–0:54**
“The loop is play, see progress, make a choice, then rest. A game reports its result, and the server saves the reward once. After three completed games, Brainy suggests a break. The character never dies or threatens the child.”

**4 · 0:54–1:12**
“You can try both roles with one tap. Each jury visitor gets a separate demo family with prepared history. It starts at nine completed learning games. Finish one more and the ten-game badge unlocks, with a celebration and a parent event.”

**5 · 1:12–1:30**
“There are two reward currencies. Stars support in-app choices such as play time and accessories. Suns come from parent-confirmed real-life tasks and can buy family gifts. This separates game activity from responsibilities that require an adult's confirmation.”

**6 · 1:30–1:48**
“A parent can assign a task on a phone while the child uses an iPad. Both see saved updates through polling, normally about every three seconds. Pairing codes connect child devices, and parents can revoke access from Settings.”

**7 · 1:48–2:06**
“English is the default, with a Russian switch. Resources load from our own domain so the product can be tested with a restricted website allowlist. Motion is brief and respects reduced-motion settings. Physical iPad verification is still a required rehearsal.”

**8 · 2:06–2:24**
“This is a working presentation MVP, not a claim of validated learning improvement. Saved games, tasks, purchases and activity totals are real. Historical radars and ranking are labelled demo data. Video media is placeholder content, and payments are not implemented.”

**9 · 2:24–2:42**
“Our next step is a small, consented family pilot. We want to learn whether children understand the choices, whether rewards support voluntary return, and whether parents can set up the product easily. We have no traction or clinical results to claim.”

**10 · 2:42–3:00**
“Please try Brainy as both a child and a parent. Complete a game, confirm a task, and watch the shared state change. Tell us what felt clear, what felt rewarding, and where you got stuck. That feedback determines what we build next.”

## Presenter guardrails
- Prepared jury history is demonstration data, never customer traction.
- Brainy stats are product feedback, not validated assessments, diagnoses or age-readiness claims.
- Do not quote seeded ranking positions or “month ago” charts as evidence of learning gains.
- No live payment, proven retention, revenue, market-size or school partnership claims.
- Show the current catalog; games are being developed separately and may change before freeze.
- Keep an online connection. The MVP does not promise cold-start offline play.
